# bot/: the CodinGame bot

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `bot.hpp` | `bot::Bot`: the decision logic. Tracker (`engine/tracker.hpp`) + Monte Carlo equity (`cpp/pe7c.hpp`) + the policy. Used by `main.cpp` and by `arena/`. |
| `main.cpp` | CodinGame entry point: `pk::read_obs` on stdin, `Bot::act`, print, flush. Builds the pe7c tables in a background thread from process start; until they are ready the bot uses the table-free `cpp/eval7_slow.hpp`. `DEBUG` prints per-turn diagnostics and first-turn platform probes to stderr; `PONDER` adds the between-turns thread probe. |
| `pf_tables.hpp` | HU jam/fold Nash thresholds per effective stack, generated by `solvers/export_pf.py` from `solvers/pf.py`. |
| `pf_rank.hpp` | Preflop class strength percentiles (combo-weighted equity against a random hand, from `solvers/eq169.bin`). |
| `bot_prev.hpp` | **Frozen** copy of `bot.hpp` (namespaces `prev`, `pf_prev`) made by `arena/freeze.py`: the arena's reference. Never edit by hand. |

## Build and submit

```sh
python3 tools/bundle.py --minify     # build/cg/poker_bundled.cpp (readable) and build/cg/poker_min.cpp (submit this)
```

Both files are compiled with CodinGame's own flags (`-std=gnu++20 -Werror=return-type -g -pthread`, no
`-O`; the sources carry `#pragma GCC optimize("O3")`; CodinGame reported `__cplusplus=202002`). Observed (M0.1): bundled 70.8k chars, minified
32.1k chars, cap 100,000. The minified binary's output on a recorded stdin is identical to the
bundled one (`tools/bundle.py` checks compilation; the output comparison was done by hand with
`sim/poker_sim.py`'s `obs_to_stdin`).

`tools/cg_minify.py` is crossfish's minifier plus `--keep NAMES` (identifiers it must not rename:
nested `std::chrono` names) and a few more std member names (`rfind`, `compare`, `what`, ...).

## Policy (M0.1)

1. The tracker replays the hand; on any mismatch it resyncs from the snapshot (never seen in the
   checks: 0 failures on 633k fuzz and 74k real decisions).
2. **Heads-up preflop at ≤ 12 BB effective: jam/fold Nash** (`pf_tables.hpp`). SB first in jams or
   folds; BB facing a jam calls or folds. 12 BB was tuned in the arena (8/12/16/25 BB tried: 12 is
   best on the mixed field and costs nothing against calling stations, which 25 did).
3. **Beliefs** (self-consistent, no opponent modelling): a bet means a strong hand. Facing a
   preflop open the opponent holds the top 35% of hands (3-bet: 20%), ranked by `pf_rank.hpp`
   (equity against a random hand). Facing a shove at a depth where our own strategy shoves
   (≤ 25 BB) they hold the Nash jam range at that depth; a deeper shove is off our tree and gets the
   uniform (ε-floor) belief. Postflop a bet of x pot means the top 65 − 35·min(1, x)% of combos by
   made-hand rank on the board. Equity is Monte Carlo against hands drawn from that range.
4. **Big calls** (≥ 40% of our stack): with 3-4 players alive, Malmuth-Harville ICM with the
   TrueSkill payouts decides (EV of call vs fold in tournament equity, with the pot capped at what
   we can win); heads-up, pot odds plus a margin of up to 0.04 at ≥ 50 BB effective (the option to
   wait for a better spot).
5. Otherwise: unopened preflop, open to 2.5 BB with equity > 0.50 (+0.03 per extra opponent), else
   complete or fold by pot odds; facing a bet, raise pot with equity > 0.75 against the range, call
   with equity > odds + 0.02, else fold; checked to, bet 0.6 pot with equity > 0.55 (+0.04 per
   extra opponent), else check.
6. Output mapping: `CALL`/`CHECK` as offered, `ALL-IN` whenever the amount covers the stack, `BET x`
   ≥ the offered minimum. 0 replaced actions in 1,000 arena games.

Arena, paired against M0 on the maniac/jammer/station/random mix with duplicate rotations
(1,905 paired games): **+0.026 ± 0.006 payout per game**, SPRT pass. Heads-up by opponent:
+0.023 vs maniac, +0.027 vs jammer, +0.007 vs random, −0.005 vs station (noise).

What M0 got wrong live (23 games, `analysis/postmortem.py`): it modelled every opponent hand as
random, so it called three streets with king-high, shoved two pair into a four-straight, and
checked down hands that a passive table would have paid; and it took 160-240 BB coin flips in
3-4-player games where ICM says fold.

Diagnostics per decision: `last_trials`, `last_equity` (against the assumed range), `last_tag`.

## What CodinGame measured (M0.1 probe, 2026-10-01)

`__cplusplus=202002`, Monte Carlo 31M trials/s (about half of this sandbox), turns 2-8 ms (15 ms
on the first), 20-230 ms between our turns. The evaluator start-up took **2.3 s** there against
80 ms here; it did not time out only because the first-turn clock starts when the referee sends the
input. Since then the tables are built in a background thread and the bot plays with
`eval7_slow` (13.5M evals/s at CodinGame flags, 0 ordering disagreements with pe7c and the engine on
300k random pairs, `cpp/test_eval7_slow.cpp`) until they are ready; the first-turn probe reports
`first_input_at_ms` and `tables_ready`.

## Probes (first turn, stderr, `DEBUG`)

`probe __cplusplus=... pe_init_ms=... mc_trials_per_s=...` then per turn `turn_ms`,
`since_last_flush_ms`, trials, equity, tag, and `DESYNC:` if the tracker resynced. With `PONDER`
a detached thread spins and each turn prints `ponder_delta`, the counter's progress since the last
turn: non-zero means the process keeps running between turns (open question Q-A1). Run the probe
build once in the CodinGame IDE, then set `PONDER = false` (and `DEBUG = false`) for submissions.
